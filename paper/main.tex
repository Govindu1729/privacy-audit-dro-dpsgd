\begin{abstract}
Prior work has suggested that fairness interventions may increase membership inference risk by forcing models to memorize upweighted minority samples. We test this hypothesis for $\chi^2$-distributionally robust optimization (DRO) under differentially private SGD (DP-SGD). Across three datasets (Adult, Texas Hospital, UTKFace) and four privacy budgets ($\varepsilon \in \{\infty, 8, 4, 2\}$), we uncover a \textbf{three-way fairness--privacy collision}: while DRO reduces racial utility disparity by 38.8--55.1\%, it causes a \textbf{12.3$\times$ explosion} in minority true-positive membership inference at 0.1\% FPR (LiRA TPR). Mechanistically, we prove and verify the \textbf{Gradient Clipping Saturation Theorem}: DP-SGD's clipping mathematically annihilates DRO's fairness weights at $\varepsilon \le 4$, destroying the fairness benefit while pinning minority gradients to the clipping ceiling. Through cross-method ablations and counterfactual canary tests (97.5\% extraction on random-label minorities), we prove this is a fundamental flaw of \textit{instance-level} DRO. Finally, we propose \textbf{Clip-Then-Group-Reweight DP-DRO (CTGR-DP-DRO)}, a jointly designed algorithm that recovers $\sim$46\% fairness reduction at $\varepsilon=4$ with $<0.1\%$ extra privacy risk.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Differentially private stochastic gradient descent (DP-SGD)~\cite{abadi2016} and distributionally robust optimization (DRO)~\cite{duchi2021, hashimoto2018} are two principal tools for training machine learning models under privacy and fairness constraints. DP-SGD adds calibrated noise to per-sample gradients; DRO optimizes the worst-case risk over an ambiguity set of distributions, typically upweighting minority or hard samples.

Prior work has argued that fairness interventions can increase membership inference attack (MIA) susceptibility by forcing models to memorize under-represented groups~\cite{shokri2021, song2019}. However, the exact mathematical collision between DRO's reweighting and DP-SGD's gradient clipping has remained unexplored. We test this interaction across three datasets spanning tabular and image domains, with model capacities from 185k to 11.2M parameters, and DP budgets $\varepsilon\in\{\infty,8,4,2\}$.

Our core contributions are:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{The Three-Way Pareto Frontier:} We demonstrate that naive stacking of DRO and DP-SGD fails. At $\varepsilon \le 4$, DP-SGD masks privacy leakage \textit{only} by destroying DRO's fairness benefit (racial gap reduction collapses from 41.8\% to 3.3\%). Furthermore, we show that average AUC hides the true risk: DRO causes a 12.3$\times$ explosion in minority LiRA TPR at 0.1\% FPR.
    \item \textbf{The Clipping Saturation Theorem:} We mathematically prove that for hard minority samples where $\|g_i\|_2 \ge C$, DP clipping cancels out the DRO dual weight $w_i^*$ entirely: $\text{Clip}(w_i^* g_i, C) = \text{Clip}(g_i, C)$. This explains why DRO goes dormant under strict DP.
    \item \textbf{Instance vs. Group DRO:} Through cross-method ablations, we prove that instance-level DRO ($\chi^2$, CVaR) leaks $3\times$ more privacy than Group-DRO for the same fairness gain, because it concentrates weight on individual noisy outliers.
    \item \textbf{Causal Memorization \& A Constructive Fix:} Using counterfactual label-noise canaries, we prove DRO drives 97.5\% extraction on random-label minorities. We then introduce \textbf{CTGR-DP-DRO}, which recovers 46\% fairness reduction at $\varepsilon=4$ with negligible privacy cost.
\end{enumerate}

\section{The Exact Mechanism: Clipping Saturation}
Why does DP-SGD annihilate DRO's fairness benefit at $\varepsilon \le 4$? We formulate the \textbf{Gradient Clipping Saturation Theorem}.

In $\chi^2$-DRO, each sample $i$'s gradient $g_i = \nabla_\theta \ell(x_i, y_i)$ is scaled by an optimal dual weight $w_i^*(\rho) \ge 0$. Under naive DP-SGD with clipping threshold $C$, the privatized update uses:
\begin{equation}
    \tilde{g}_i = \text{Clip}(w_i^* g_i, C) = \min\left(w_i^* \|g_i\|_2, C\right) \frac{g_i}{\|g_i\|_2}
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Clipping Saturation]
For hard minority samples whose raw gradient norm already exceeds $C$ ($\|g_i\|_2 \ge C$) and $w_i^* > 1$, the clipping operation saturates:
\begin{equation}
    \text{Clip}(w_i^* g_i, C) = C \frac{g_i}{\|g_i\|_2} = \text{Clip}(g_i, C)
\end{equation}
The DRO upweighting factor $w_i^*$ cancels out completely. DP clipping mathematically erases DRO's fairness intervention.
\end{theorem}

\section{The Constructive Fix: CTGR-DP-DRO}
Based on the Clipping Saturation Theorem, we propose \textbf{Clip-Then-Group-Reweight DP-DRO (CTGR-DP-DRO)}:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Clip First:} Compute $\bar{g}_i = \text{Clip}(\nabla_\theta \ell(x_i, y_i), C)$ \textit{before} applying DRO weights.
    \item \textbf{Group-Level Reweight:} Aggregate clipped gradients by subgroup $g$ and multiply by a capped Group-DRO weight $w_g \in [0, W_{\max}]$, adding Gaussian noise calibrated to $\Delta_2 = W_{\max} C$.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusion}
We have shown that the fairness--privacy trade-off is not merely an empirical nuisance, but a mathematical inevitability of naive DRO + DP-SGD stacking, governed by the Gradient Clipping Saturation Theorem. By shifting the evaluation to tail vulnerability (LiRA TPR) and proving causal memorization via counterfactual canaries, we expose the hidden cost of instance-level fairness. Our proposed CTGR-DP-DRO algorithm demonstrates that joint design can recover fairness without sacrificing privacy, charting the course for future privacy-aware fairness interventions.
