# Privacy Audit Methodology — Grounded in Published MIA Methods

This document grounds the project's privacy-audit notebooks in the attack-AUC
methodology of two published membership inference attack (MIA) papers that are
already indexed in the local literature index. It is intended as a review
artifact before any notebook code is changed.

## (a) Papers reviewed

| doc_id (index) | Title | arXiv ID |
|---|---|---|
| `arxiv_GLiRA__Black-Box_Membership_Inference_Attack_via_Knowledge_Distillation` | GLiRA: Black-Box Membership Inference Attack via Knowledge Distillation (Galichin et al.) | arXiv:2405.07562 |
| `arxiv_Imitative_Membership_Inference_Attack` | Imitative Membership Inference Attack (Du, Chen, Xiao, Ribeiro, Li) | arXiv:2509.06796 |

Both papers evaluate black-box MIAs on image classification benchmarks
(MNIST, FMNIST, CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, CINIC-10) and compare against LiRA,
Calibration, Canary, RMIA, RAPID and related baselines.

## (b) Shadow model training convention

The standard convention, as stated in the IMIA paper, is *shadow training*:
"training multiple shadow models on datasets drawn from the same distribution
as the target model's training set. For each query instance, the membership
scores generated by the shadow models trained without the instance
(shadow-out models) represent the out-distribution for the instance."
(`arxiv_Imitative_Membership_Inference_Attack`, chunk 1)

Two concrete conventions follow from this:

1. **Data**: shadow models are trained on subsets drawn from the same
   distribution as the target's training data (a hold-out dataset resembling
   the target's training distribution). GLiRA: "We train shadow models on
   different subsets of the hold-out dataset, resembling the offline
   membership inference attack." (`arxiv_GLiRA__...`, chunk 2).
2. **Architecture/capacity**: "the shadow models should be of the same or more
   complicated architecture than the target model to ensure high efficiency of
   the membership inference attack." (`arxiv_GLiRA__...`, chunk 2). GLiRA
   additionally trains each shadow model via knowledge distillation from the
   target model (target as teacher, shadow as student) so that the shadow
   models mimic the target's output distribution rather than just the data
   labels (`arxiv_GLiRA__...`, chunks 2 and 11, Algorithm 1 "Distill").
3. **Score construction**: the attack score is a likelihood-ratio /
   out-of-sample statistic. In the offline setting GLiRA collects
   confidences from N shadow-out models, fits a Gaussian
   N(mu_out, sigma_out^2), and scores the observed target confidence as
   `1 - p(conf_obs | N(mu_out, sigma_out^2))` (`arxiv_GLiRA__...`, chunk 11,
   Algorithm 2). IMIA similarly uses shadow-out models for the
   out-distribution and shadow-in models for the in-distribution
   (`arxiv_Imitative_Membership_Inference_Attack`, chunk 1).

**Implication for our audit notebooks**: any shadow-model pipeline must (i)
draw shadow training data from a distribution matching the target's training
data (not from the test set), (ii) use at least the target's architecture,
and (iii) compute per-query scores from models that did NOT see the query
point (shadow-out), optionally with shadow-in models for the in-distribution.

## (c) Attack AUC reporting convention

Both papers report a *panel* of metrics rather than a single number:

- **AUC** of the ROC curve (membership score vs. ground-truth membership).
  GLiRA explicitly notes: "In practice, AUC is not very informative when
  measuring the success rate of a membership inference attack. It is reported
  to ensure the completeness of the comparison with the other works."
  (`arxiv_GLiRA__...`, chunk 14).
- **TPR at fixed low FPR** (the primary metric): "Following the work [35], we
  report the values of the true positive rate (TPR) at the fixed low value of
  the false positive rate (FPR)." (`arxiv_GLiRA__...`, chunk 14). Reported
  operating points include TPR @ 0.01% FPR, TPR @ 0.1% FPR, and TPR @ 1% FPR
  (`arxiv_GLiRA__...`, chunk 14, Table I; `arxiv_Imitative_Membership_Inference_Attack`,
  chunk 61).
- **Balanced accuracy** as a threshold-free secondary metric
  (`arxiv_Imitative_Membership_Inference_Attack`, chunk 35, Table 16).

**Implication for our audit notebooks**: reporting AUC alone is insufficient
and can be misleading. The corrected evaluation plan must report
TPR @ {0.01%, 0.1%, 1%} FPR alongside AUC and balanced accuracy, and must
compute the ROC/AUC from the continuous attack score (not from a hard
threshold).

## (d) DP epsilon vs. empirical attack AUC

The IMIA paper evaluates attacks against DP-SGD-trained targets and reports
the relationship empirically (Table 16, `arxiv_Imitative_Membership_Inference_Attack`,
chunk 35):

- Target: ResNet on CIFAR-10 trained with DP-SGD, clipping norm C = 10.
- As the noise multiplier sigma increases (privacy guarantee strengthens,
  i.e., epsilon decreases), attack performance degrades monotonically:
  - No defense (epsilon = infinity): model acc 90.41%, TPR @ 0.1% FPR = 3.42%
  - sigma = 0.2 (epsilon > 1000): acc 49.76%, TPR @ 0.1% FPR = 0.32%
  - sigma = 0.5 (epsilon = 31): acc 37.55%, TPR @ 0.1% FPR = 0.15%
  - sigma = 1.0 (epsilon = 4): acc 32.10%, TPR @ 0.1% FPR = 0.12%
- The paper's stated observation: "as the privacy guarantee strengthens, all
  attacks experience reduced performance, and their performance gaps narrow.
  Nevertheless, IMIA consistently outperforms existing MIAs, especially in the
  low false-positive regime." (`arxiv_Imitative_Membership_Inference_Attack`,
  chunk 35).
- Balanced accuracy of all attacks collapses toward 50% (chance) as sigma
  grows from 0.2 to 0.5 (Table 16), i.e., the empirical ROC degrades toward
  the diagonal as epsilon shrinks.

**Implication for our audit notebooks**: the empirical attack AUC (and
TPR@low-FPR) is a *monotonically decreasing* function of DP strength
(decreasing epsilon). A useful audit should therefore sweep several
(sigma, epsilon) pairs, report TPR @ low FPR and balanced accuracy at each,
and treat "balanced accuracy ≈ 50% / AUC ≈ 0.5" as the empirical signature of
a well-defended model. Reporting a single AUC at one epsilon without the
low-FPR operating points understates the attack's practical risk.

---

### Corrected evaluation plan (summary)

1. Build shadow models on data from the target's training distribution, with
   architecture >= target, optionally KD-trained from the target
   (sections b, GLiRA / IMIA conventions).
2. Score each query with shadow-out (and, where available, shadow-in)
   models; never with a model that saw the query point.
3. Report AUC **and** TPR @ {0.01%, 0.1%, 1%} FPR **and** balanced accuracy;
   do not rely on AUC alone.
4. For DP-trained targets, sweep (sigma, epsilon) and report the metric panel
   at each point; expect monotonic degradation toward chance as epsilon
   decreases.

## Gaps vs the current notebook

Concrete deviations of `final_audit.ipynb` from the published conventions
above:

1. **No shadow models are trained at all.** The notebook loads pre-computed
   `bb_results.pkl` / `wb_results.pkl` (16 black-box and 7 white-box shadow
   score matrices) and hard-codes fallback numbers; it never trains shadow
   models on data drawn from the target's training distribution, never checks
   that the shadow architecture is >= the target's, and never uses
   knowledge-distillation training from the target. The published convention
   requires shadow models trained on same-distribution subsets (shadow-out /
   shadow-in) as the source of the membership scores
   (`arxiv_Imitative_Membership_Inference_Attack` chunk 1;
   `arxiv_GLiRA__...` chunks 2, 11). With only 7–16 shadows and no documented
   training protocol, the score distributions in the notebook cannot be
   validated against the published methodology.

2. **The primary metric is not the published one.** The notebook reports a
   single "epsilon lower bound" derived from a k-of-r canary correctness count
   (e.g., 305/530 correct at k=265) and a Mann-Whitney U test, but never
   computes ROC AUC, TPR @ {0.01%, 0.1%, 1%} FPR, or balanced accuracy from
   the continuous shadow scores — the metric panel both papers use
   (`arxiv_GLiRA__...` chunk 14; `arxiv_Imitative_Membership_Inference_Attack`
   chunk 35). The score matrices (`shadow_scores` with IN/OUT membership
   labels) are already in the pickle files, so the published metric panel can
   be computed directly without retraining; the notebook currently only plots
   histograms.

3. **No epsilon sweep / DP-budget linkage.** The notebook evaluates a single
   DP-SGD model (claimed epsilon = 0.37) and compares one black-box and one
   white-box lower bound against it. The published convention evaluates attack
   performance across a range of (sigma, epsilon) pairs and reports the
   monotonic degradation of TPR @ low FPR and balanced accuracy toward chance
   as epsilon decreases (`arxiv_Imitative_Membership_Inference_Attack` chunk
   35, Table 16). The corrected plan should audit multiple
   (sigma, epsilon) checkpoints and report the full metric panel at each,
   rather than a single-point comparison.
